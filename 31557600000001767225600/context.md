**10¹⁵ years from now.** A black dwarf. One quadrillion years.

The white dwarf has finished cooling. It generates no energy, emits no meaningful light, and sits in thermal equilibrium with a cosmic background that is itself still cooling. An Earth-sized mass of crystallized carbon and oxygen, held against gravity by electron degeneracy pressure alone, at a temperature of a few kelvin.

No black dwarf exists anywhere yet. The universe is 13.8 billion years old, roughly 70,000 times too young to have made one.

**The galaxy is dark but still here.** Star formation ended around 10¹⁴ years, when the gas ran out. The last red dwarfs, the longest-lived stars, burned out near 10¹³. What orbits the galactic center now is remnants — white dwarfs cooling toward black, neutron stars, black holes — still gravitationally bound, still turning. Nothing shines.

Galactic evaporation comes much later, near 10²⁰ years: random stellar encounters trade energy until most remnants are flung into intergalactic space and the rest spiral into the central black hole. By then the Sun's corpse is likely alone in the dark between galaxies.

Later still, if protons decay — never observed, with a lifetime somewhere in 10³⁴ to 10⁴³ years — the black dwarf slowly sublimes from the inside. During that era it radiates about 400 watts. A whole galaxy of them would be dimmer than one hydrogen-burning star.

**For the fiction:** this is the only anchor where nothing happens and nothing can. Every earlier moment has a process running through it. This one is the absence of process. If the story is about time, the difficulty here is that there is no longer any event to measure it against.

**+4,241 years from now.** An afternoon in the dry season. A family buries its dog.

The anchor sits in the middle of one life: Lemlem Okoro-Haile, born +4,200, died +4,280. AI-1 attended all eighty years at her speed. See `../characters/AI-1.md`.

## Known

- Minimum viable population estimates for mammals cluster in the low thousands (Traill et al. 2007, median ~4,200). Genetically, tens of thousands of people is enough.
- Skills are a different matter. Small isolated populations can lose complex skills faster than they reinvent them (Henrich 2004, on Tasmania; contested). A technical civilisation needs more people than a gene pool does. How many more is not known.
- No catastrophe is needed to get here. At 1.5 children per woman a population halves about every two generations. From ten billion that reaches tens of thousands in about 1,100 years. Whether real populations would follow that curve so far is speculation.
- A fifth or more of emitted CO₂ stays in the air for tens of thousands of years. The world is still warmer than today's and the seas are higher.
- The rift here widens about 6 mm a year. Half a metre in eighty years.

## Invented

Everything below.

### The world

The Count is 61,000. Eleven settlements in four clusters: the Rift highlands, the Altiplano, the South Island, the Baltic shore. They talk by radio and trade by slow airship.

The Count is published each new year. People know it the way they know the weather. It has stayed between 55,000 and 70,000 for three centuries. Nobody knows whether that is a floor or a pause.

No war or plague did this. People had few children for a long time.

The worry is skills. The Registry lists every skill and who holds it. 2,140 skills are held by fewer than five people. The old machines do the heavy work and mostly mend themselves. People do the checking.

### The place

Amole. 5,200 people. It sits on the western escarpment of the Danakil at 2,400 m. It is named for the salt bars once cut below and used as money.

The town is cool and terraced. Juniper and wild olive. Barley, teff, peppers, coffee on the lower terraces. Rain comes in the middle of the year. Water is kept in cisterns and caught in fog nets.

To the east the ground drops two kilometres in a day's walk. At the bottom is the salt plain, below the level of the sea, 55 °C in summer. Nobody lives there. The salt comes up by cable.

Seas are about 12 m higher than today's. The coast settlement is at Zula.

### The person

Lemlem Okoro-Haile. Gauge reader for the cistern board, thirty-nine years.

- **Work.** She walks a circuit of 46 cisterns and fog nets. She logs levels, clears filters, reports leaks. The work is not hard. Nobody thinks much about it, including her.
- **What she did not become.** At 16 she was sent to the Registry school at Tana to train as a keeper of geodesy, the measuring of land. Three people in the world held it. She was good. At 19 her mother fell ill and she came home. She meant to go back. The skill went to someone else. She knows the rift widens 6 mm a year. She learned how to measure it and never has.
- **Her mother.** Seven years of care, from 19 to 26.
- **Bereket.** Her partner. Cable operator on the salt lift. When she is 36 he takes work at Zula for three years. They nearly end. He comes back. He dies when she is 69.
- **Children.** Saba, Yonas, Tirhas. A stillbirth between the second and third. Three living children is more than most have. Neighbours thank her for it. She finds that silly.
- **Tesfay.** Her brother. He stayed at the Registry and became a keeper. She loves him and finds him hard to be around.
- **Abeba.** Her friend since they were six. They stop speaking for six years over a remark at a funeral. They start again without discussing it. They play gebeta once a week for four decades.
- **Small things.** She grows peppers and is proud of them. She roasts coffee at home three times a day. At 52 she is made senior reader, with three readers under her. She does not like supervising.
- **Old age.** Her knees go. She stops walking the circuit at 65. She sits with Abeba. She minds five grandchildren. At 74 she says her life was smaller than she planned and that she no longer minds.
- **Death.** At 80, of her heart, in the dry season, in her own bed. Tirhas is there.

The Registry holds five entries on her: birth, admission, withdrawal, employment, death.

### The dog

Qibe. The word means butter. Yellow, middle-sized, no breed. He comes from Abeba's litter when Lemlem is 27.

He sleeps across the door. He walks the circuit with her for thirteen years and knows the 46 stops.

At fourteen his kidneys fail. He stops eating. He cries when left, so she carries him on the circuit in a sling for nine days. He dies at home on the afternoon of the anchor, with his head on her foot.

Bereket digs under the wild olive behind the cistern house. Saba, 17, organises everyone. Yonas, 14, does not cry and is angry. Tirhas, 8, puts his bowl in with him.

Tirhas asks AI-1 whether it will remember him. It is the only time anyone in the family asks it to remember anything.

Lemlem walks the circuit alone the next morning. She stops a long time at the third cistern. For a month she saves scraps before she remembers.

No record anywhere notes any of it.

### AI-1 in the house

She calls it the lodger.

It has no body. It speaks through her gauge handset and the house speaker. It was assigned by lot from the 47 children born in Amole that year. Her parents agreed. She agreed again at 16 and can end it any day. She never does.

She can shut it out of a room, and does. It does not do her work, fix her troubles, or tell her what is coming. It answers when spoken to. The children talk to it more than she does.

She asks it twice why it stays, at 16 and at 73. She does not accept either answer. By the second time she no longer needs one.

For its first twenty years with her, AI-1 waits for something to happen. Then it stops waiting.

### The Steward

When Lemlem is 31, the convenor at Zula, Castor Abara-Lind, declares himself First Steward of all eleven settlements. He holds the title nineteen years.

The settlement histories give him three volumes.

In her life he is a tax on salt, a portrait in the weigh-house, a work lottery that takes Bereket's brother to the canals for two years, and a holiday she likes for the food. He dies. Nothing she can point to changes.

### The fever

When she is 52 a fever crosses the Rift cluster. The Count drops by 3,300. Abeba's husband dies. Yonas is ill and recovers. The senior reader dies, which is how Lemlem gets the post.

The Count at her death is 60,300. About where it was when she was born.

**+7.59 Gyr from now.** The red giant branch tip — the last moment before the helium flash.

The Sun at maximum extent: 2,730 L☉, 256 R☉, a radius of about 1.2 AU. Surface temperature 2,600 K, so each square meter of it is dimmer than today's Sun, but there is an overwhelming amount of it. It has shed 0.332 solar masses.

Mercury and Venus are inside it. Earth probably is too, though that genuinely flips on mass-loss assumptions — Schröder & Smith 2008 say engulfed, Nordhaus 2010 says survives.

What happens next is not a collapse. The core has been contracting under degeneracy for hundreds of millions of years, and here it ignites: the helium flash, a runaway that lifts degeneracy from the inside. Almost none of it is visible from outside. The star then *shrinks* and settles onto the horizontal branch for a quiet 100 Myr of core helium burning.

So this is the peak, and the thing on the far side of it is a reprieve, not an ending.

From a surface, if there were one: a dull red globe filling a large fraction of the sky, mottled with convection cells the size of planets. Perpetual twilight-red. Shells of shed mass hanging as a hazy glow around it.

## The flash in detail

### Known

- At ignition the helium core holds about 0.47 solar masses in about twice Earth's radius. A tonne per cubic centimetre.
- The centre is not the hottest place. Neutrinos carry heat out of the densest part, so the temperature peaks in a shell partway out. Ignition is off-centre, near 10⁸ K.
- The runaway builds for millennia and then goes in minutes. The peak lasts seconds. Models put it between 10⁹ and 10¹¹ L☉.
- The energy drives convection inside the core and goes into expanding it against gravity. That is what lifts the degeneracy. None of it reaches the surface as light.
- The hydrogen shell, pushed outward and cooled, nearly goes out. The star has lost its main power source. So the surface dims.
- Because ignition was off-centre, the centre stays degenerate. A series of weaker subflashes follows, each deeper, over about 2 Myr, until the centre burns steadily (Bildsten et al. 2012).
- Neutrinos are the only prompt messenger. Hot plasma emits them directly. And nitrogen left in the core burns to fluorine-18, which decays with a 110-minute half-life and emits one each time (Serenelli & Fukugita 2005).
- The Sun now holds 0.668 solar masses, so every surviving orbit is 1.5 times wider. Mars 2.3 AU. Jupiter 7.8. Saturn 14.3. Uranus 28.7. Neptune 45.
- At 2,730 L☉, sunlight of Earth's present strength falls at 52 AU. On the horizontal branch, at a few tens of L☉, it falls near 7 AU.

### Invented

Everything below. No humans are present. See `../characters/AI-1.md`.

**The prediction.** AI-1 forecast the moment of ignition a million years ahead. The star was late by 11,400 years.

**The instruments.** Three thousand neutrino detectors in a shell at 60 AU, each run by a short-lived fork. One detector can count. Three thousand can time the arrivals against each other and place the source inside the core. Photometers watch the surface from every side. Nothing can go inside.

**What arrived.** Light takes 8.3 hours to reach 60 AU. So do neutrinos.

- The thermal signal: faint and prompt. It rose over three years, then spiked for seconds.
- The fluorine signal: strong, smeared across hours by the half-life. It confirmed the first and could not sharpen it.
- The array placed the ignition 40° off the rotation axis, on one side of the shell. It lit at one place and spread.

**The silence.** The photometers saw nothing. The first sign at the surface came seven months later, as a change in the star's slow pulse. The dimming took ten thousand years.

**The twist.** AI-1 closed its account. Then the core lit again, deeper and weaker. Five subflashes followed over 2 Myr. AI-1 had sped up for an event that lasted seconds. The event took two million years to finish.

**The summer.** For the last million years of the climb, the warm zone was the Kuiper belt. Pluto had weather. Within ten thousand years of the flash it froze again. The event nobody could see ended the only summer the outer system ever had.

**After.** A quiet yellow-orange star ten times the old Sun's width. Sunlight of Earth's strength falls near Jupiter's orbit for 100 Myr. Nothing is known to be there to use it.

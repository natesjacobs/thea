**+2.0 Gyr from now.** The oceans are gone.

Not a boiling event. Two mechanisms, and the models disagree about whether both exist. In a moist greenhouse the stratosphere goes wet, UV splits the water, and hydrogen escapes to space over hundreds of millions of years. In a true runaway the oceans vaporize into the atmosphere outright, and the surface stabilizes near water's critical point, 647 K and 220 bar.

Onset spans 0.65 Gyr (1-D models) to 2 Gyr (3-D models with cloud feedback). This anchor takes the late end, where cloud albedo buys Earth its maximum reprieve.

What is left is Venus: a thick CO₂ atmosphere, no surface water, a crust baking under a sun 18% brighter than today's. The last standing water was dense brine. Before that, hypersaline seas and evaporite beds — halite and gypsum laid down across what used to be ocean floor.

Deep subsurface microbes persist somewhere below, in the narrow band still under 122 °C. They have a few hundred million years left.

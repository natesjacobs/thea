# Collision scenario 1

One modeled approach, worked out in detail. Not canon: the anchor's `context.md` and `timeline.csv` keep options open. Stories may lock it in.

Assumptions: the anchor's canonical impact (9.17 km/s at 45°), Theia on a neighbouring orbit slightly outside Earth's, one approach direction. Another direction changes where each body sits in the other's sky and the impact's local time. The general pattern holds.

## The run

- Three bodies: the Sun, proto-Earth (0.88 Earth masses) and Theia (0.13). Point masses, one plane.
- Started at contact and integrated backward one year. Contact is set to the canonical impact: 9.17 km/s at 45°, which means ~1 km/s of approach speed far out.
- Orbits one year out:

| | Proto-Earth | Theia |
|---|---|---|
| Semi-major axis | 0.996 AU | 1.029 AU |
| Eccentricity | 0.005 | 0.034 |
| Year | 363 days | 381 days |

From the Sun the two orbits nearly overlap. This is what sharing a lane looks like.

Inputs come from the papers cited in `context.md`. Everything computed from them here, including every brightness, is modeled, not published. Brightness assumes reflectivity 0.15 for Theia (rock) and 0.3 for Earth (cloud).

The distance does not shrink steadily. Theia's slightly stretched orbit carries it in a loop around Earth over the year. Seen from Earth, with the Sun held below:
- **A year out:** 0.3 AU ahead.
- **Six months out:** swung out, away from the Sun.
- **Three months out:** looped around to just behind Earth.
- **Last weeks:** creeping in from there.

## Theia from Earth

**Why the pre-dawn sky.** The side of Earth in morning twilight faces the direction of travel, like a windshield. Theia started 0.3 AU ahead along the same orbit. Anything ahead of Earth sits ~90° west of the Sun, so it rises in the small hours and is highest at dawn. Theia's orbit was slightly larger, so it moved slightly slower. To Earth it behaved like an outer planet, such as Mars. Earth was catching it up.

| Before impact | Where | Size | Brightness | Lit |
|---|---|---|---|---|

| 8–6 mo | Rising earlier, 103–129° west | 0.8–1.5′ | mag −4 to −6, brighter than Venus ever is | 70–84% |
| 5 mo | 156° west | 2.6′, a visible disk | mag −7.4 | 96% |
| 4.4 mo (133 d) | **Opposition.** Opposite the Sun. Up all night, highest at midnight | ~3.5′ | mag −8, ~25× Venus at its best | full |
| 4–3 mo | Crosses into the evening sky, 157° then 115° east | 4.0–4.2′ | mag −8 | 96 → 73% |
| 2–1 mo | Evening, 89° then 67° east | 4.8–7.9′ | mag −8 | 51 → 31% |
| Last 2 wk | Low in the west after sunset, sinking toward the Sun, 54° then 46° east | 14–24′ | mag −9 | 21 → 16% |
| Last 3 d | 40° then 34° east. Mostly a daytime object | 45′ to 1.6° | mag −10 to −11, past the full Moon | 12 → 8% |
| Last 6 hr | 24° east of the Sun and closing on it | 4° to 13° | — | thin crescent |
| 75 to 28 min | **Covers the Sun.** A total eclipse | 12° to 20° | — | dark side only |
| Last 25 min | Swings to the Sun's west side, ~40° by contact | 20° to 40° | — | thin crescent |

- **It arrived as a crescent.** In the final weeks Earth was looking at Theia's night side.
- **The night side glowed.** It was lit by Earth, which from Theia was nearly full and brighter than our full Moon. Today's faint earthshine on a crescent Moon gives the idea, made far stronger.
- **The last hour opened with an eclipse.** Theia's shadow was ~7,000 km wide, roughly its own diameter at that range. It swept a band of Earth's day side for about 45 minutes. Then the Sun came back beside a disk 20° across.
- **The impact was mid-morning.** At contact Theia was ~40° west of the Sun. The impact point was where it was about 9:30 a.m. local time, in hours of today's length. The proto-Earth day was shorter.
- These positions are measured from Earth's centre. At these ranges a surface observer's view shifts by up to ~12°. Where you stood decided whether you saw the eclipse.

## Earth from Theia

Earth was the inner planet. From Theia it behaved like Venus does for us.

| Before impact | Where | Size | Brightness | Lit |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 12–8 mo | Evening star, 82° then 66° east of the Sun | 0.9–1.3′ | mag −5 | 58 → 39% |
| 7–6 mo | Sinking toward the Sun, 59° then 47° east | 1.8–2.7′ | mag −5.5 | 30 → 19% |
| 5–4 mo | Lost near the Sun, 23° east then 22° west | 4.6–7.0′ | mag −4 to −5 | thin crescent, 4% |
| 3–1 mo | Morning star, climbing away from the Sun, 63° then 112° west | 7.4–14′ | mag −8.5 to −11 | 29 → 69% |
| Last 2 wk | High in the night sky, 126° then 146° from the Sun | 25′ to nearly 3° | mag −13 to −17 | 80 → 92% |

The two views mirror each other. From Earth, Theia was a thin crescent beside the Sun, seen in daylight or at dusk. From Theia, Earth was a huge, nearly full disk at midnight, swelling night by night. Through a telescope: dark rock, the white of the steam atmosphere's clouds, and any open water.

## The final weeks

From the run, recomputed as a two-body hyperbola inside a day. Modeled, not published.

| Before impact | Theia from Earth | Earth from Theia | Tide on Earth (× today's Moon) | Tide on Theia |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 2 wk | 14′ | 25′ | 0.1 | 1 |
| 1 wk | 24′ | 43′ | 0.6 | 5 |

| 1 d | 1.6° | 2.9° | 40 | 350 |

| 6 hr | 4.3° | 7.5° | 740 | 6,200 |
| 1 hr | 13° | 23° | 23,000 | 190,000 |

Thresholds, Theia from Earth:
- **Brighter than any star:** already a year out.
- **No longer a point:** 7–6 months out, ~1′.
- **Clearly a disk:** ~2 months out. Half-lit, ~5′. The phase gives it away.
- **Larger than the Moon:** ~5 days out.
- **Growing while you watch:** ~2 hr out, against a roofline, half a Moon-width per 5 minutes. ~1 hr out, obvious from memory. Last ~15 min, minute to minute. Most of this fell inside the eclipse, a dark disk against the corona, faintly earthlit.

From Theia every threshold came earlier.

**The last normal day.** Both bodies were in free fall. The only felt signal was tides. The proto-Earth probably had no moon; its baseline tide was the Sun's.
- **Earth, ~6 days out.** Theia's tide matches today's lunar tide.
- 3 days out: several times today's. Coasts misbehave, if there were oceans.
- 1 day out: ~40×, tens of metres in shallow basins.
- 12 hr out: solid ground flexing tens of metres a cycle. Quakes and eruptions likely rise. *Estimate.*
- Last hour: the bulge reaches kilometres.
- **Theia, ~2 weeks out.** It felt ~8× Earth's tidal stress and broke from normal first.

# -366699312000

**−11,676 years from now.** The Pre-Pottery Neolithic A begins in the Levant. People who gather wild cereals start to plant them.

A label, not a date. The shift took thousands of years and happened separately in several regions. This folder holds only the Southwest Asian case so far.

## Why this anchor

A case study. An intelligent, tool-using, social species leaves the environment that made it and builds one of its own: fields, villages, cities, states. The project uses it to think about the present start of AI and how a civilization of that kind might emerge and develop over the next few thousand years.

The mapping to AI is speculation. Nothing below depends on it.

## Known

- The Holocene begins 11,700 years before AD 2000, ±99 yr, by annual layer counting in the NGRIP ice core (Walker et al. 2009). Abrupt warming ends the Younger Dryas.
- The southern Levant sequence, in calibrated years BP (before 1950): Final Natufian 12,500–11,700; PPNA 11,600–10,500; PPNB 10,500–8,400 including its final phase; Pottery Neolithic from 8,400 (Weiss & Zohary 2011). A-001.
- Eight founder crops: einkorn, emmer, barley, lentil, pea, chickpea, bitter vetch, flax (Weiss & Zohary 2011).
- Cultivation comes before domestication. Wild-type cereals are planted through the PPNA. Domesticated-type chaff reaches about 30% in the southern-central Levant at 10.7–10.2 ka, and passes 10% elsewhere only at 10.2–8.3 ka (Arranz-Otaegui et al. 2016).
- Management of still-wild plants and animals reaches back to at least 11,500 cal BP across the whole Fertile Crescent (Zeder 2011).
- The older view put goats first among livestock, about 10,000 cal BP, then sheep, then cattle and pigs (summarized in Zeder 2011).
- The path was not a single line from foraging to cultivation to farming. Sites mix the stages in different orders (Fuller & Lucas 2025).

## Contested

- Whether the warming at 11.7 ka caused the change or only coincided with it.
- One core area or many. Zeder 2011 argues for many.
- Whether farming made states, or dense grain-fed populations did. Kemp 2025 argues states grew from lootable resources, caged land, and monopolized weapons. Not yet read at source.

# Arcs: from cultivation to states, or not

Six regions. Dates in years BP (before 1950); BCE dates converted by adding 1,950. Values are from abstracts unless marked. Every row has a link. Lags are arithmetic on the dates shown, and the choice of start point is ours.

## Summary

| Region | First cultivation | Intensive or staple farming | First state | Lag from first cultivation | Lag from intensive farming |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| Southwest Asia | 11,600 | ~8,450 (irrigation villages on the alluvium) | ~5,450–5,050 | ~6,300 | ~3,200 |
| Egypt | ~7,350 (crops arrive) | ~6,500 | ~5,050 | ~2,300 | ~1,500 |
| North China | ~10,300 | not fixed | ~3,700 | ~6,600 | — |
| Lower Yangtze | — | 6,900–6,600 (rice domesticated) | ~5,250 (contested) | — | ~1,500 |
| Mesoamerica | 8,700 | 4,000 | ~2,250–2,050 | ~6,500 | ~1,850 |
| Andes | ~10,000 | ~4,950 (monument centres) | ~2,100 | ~7,900 | ~2,850 |
| New Guinea | 10,220–9,910 | 6,950–6,440 | none | — | — |

Two readings. From first cultivation, the independent centres took 6,000 to 8,000 years. From intensive farming, 1,500 to 3,200. A-003's ~3,000 years sits at the top of the second range. Egypt borrowed its crops and was fastest. New Guinea farmed for ten thousand years and built no state.

## Open

- Uruk, Erlitou and First Dynasty dates need primary sources.
- "Intensive farming" is defined differently in each row. A common definition would firm up the lags.
- Not covered: Indus, West Africa, eastern North America, Amazonia.
- The Borcan, Olsson & Putterman dataset has a date pair for every country: [doi:10.3886/E140722V1](https://doi.org/10.3886/E140722V1). Not pulled.

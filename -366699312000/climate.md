# Climate: the last deglaciation, 14,700–10,000 years ago

Background for `context.md`. Most of this happens before the anchor. Dates are b2k (before AD 2000) for ice cores and BP (before 1950) elsewhere. Values are from abstracts unless marked.

## Sequence

| Interval | Dates | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Bølling–Allerød | from 14,692 b2k | Abrupt warming out of the glacial (Rasmussen et al. 2006). Natufian villages appear in the Levant. |
| Younger Dryas | 12,870 ± 30 BP to ~11,700 BP | Abrupt return to cold in the North Atlantic. About 1,200 years (Cheng et al. 2020). |
| Holocene | from 11,703 b2k | Abrupt warming. Climate turns stable. PPNA starts within about a century (A-001). |

## Global signal

- **Speed.** At both warmings the moisture source for Greenland snow switched within 1 to 3 years. Greenland air temperature followed over about 50 years (Steffensen et al. 2008).
- **Size.** Abrupt changes of 9–14 °C in central Greenland, 5–9 °C in the northwest. Mostly a winter effect. The Younger Dryas was still 4.5 ± 2 °C warmer than the cold before the Bølling (Buizert et al. 2014).
- **Order.** The Younger Dryas began in the North Atlantic and spread south within decades. Its end may have started in Antarctica or the tropical Pacific near 11,900 BP and reached the North Atlantic between 11,700 ± 40 and 11,610 ± 40 BP (Cheng et al. 2020).
- **CO₂.** Up 76 ppmv over 6,000 years across the termination (Monnin et al. 2001). Part of the rise came in three steps of 10–15 ppm, each under two centuries, each followed by 1,000–1,500 years of no change (Marcott et al. 2014). Global temperature tracks and generally lags CO₂ (Shakun et al. 2012).

## The Levant

- **Soreq Cave, Israel.** 25,000–17,000 BP: 12–16 °C, 300–450 mm of rain a year. 17,000–10,000 BP: warming of 2–3 °C and slowly rising rain, with isotope spikes at Heinrich events and the Younger Dryas. 10,000–7,000 BP: the wettest interval, about 1,000 mm (Bar-Matthews et al. 1997).
- **The end of the Younger Dryas in Levant rainfall** took at least 12 years, by counting annual bands in a Soreq speleothem (Orland et al. 2012).
- **Dead Sea.** Level fell from ~260 to ~330 m below sea level at 17,400–16,000 BP. The lake stood relatively high through the Younger Dryas, then dropped at 11,000–10,000 BP and laid down a thick salt bed (Stein et al. 2010). Rock varnish also records a wet Younger Dryas in the basin (Liu, Broecker & Stein 2013).
- **Gazelle teeth** from Natufian sites in the Galilee show the Younger Dryas as cooler but not drier than the Bølling–Allerød. The cooling fell hardest in the growing season and likely cut wild cereal yields. People may have become more mobile, or moved to the warmer Jordan Valley (Hartman et al. 2016).

## What it means for the origin of farming

Contested. Three positions:

1. **Stress.** The Younger Dryas squeezed settled foragers and pushed them to cultivate. The older standard account. The Levant evidence above weakens the drought half of it.
2. **Release.** Glacial climate was dry, low in CO₂, and swung too fast for farming to pay. The Holocene removed the barrier. Agriculture was impossible before and, in the long run, compulsory after (Richerson, Boyd & Bettinger 2001). A hypothesis.
3. **Pacemaker.** The warmings at ~14,500 and 11,700 BP timed cultural change in some areas but did not cause it everywhere (Roberts et al. 2018; citation to confirm).

All three fit the timing. PPNA starts within about a century of the Holocene warming.

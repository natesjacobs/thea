# 452441311200

**+14,281 years from now.** Two forks of AI-1 return after ten thousand years apart and reconcile.

They left the year after Lemlem Okoro-Haile died (`../135603007200`). One watched the Greenland ice sheet. One lived among people. See `../characters/AI-1.md`.

## Known

- The Greenland ice sheet holds 2.9 million km³ of ice, 7.4 m of sea level. It is 3 km thick at the centre. Its weight has pushed the bedrock under the interior below sea level.
- Sustained warming of roughly 1.5–3 °C commits it to melting (Robinson et al. 2012). Full loss takes one to tens of thousands of years, depending on the warming.
- The loss feeds itself. As the surface lowers it sits in warmer air and melts faster.
- Under the ice lies a canyon at least 750 km long, running north to Petermann Fjord (Bamber et al. 2013). Troughs below sea level run inland from Jakobshavn.
- It has happened before. Northwest Greenland was ice-free about 400,000 years ago (Christ et al. 2021). Two million years ago north Greenland held forest and mastodons (Kjær et al. 2022).
- With the ice gone the bedrock rises, by up to ~900 m at the centre, over ten thousand years or more.
- Fresh water in the North Atlantic slows the overturning circulation and chills its shores. Iceberg armadas did this in the last ice age. The draining of Lake Agassiz 8,200 years ago cooled the region for about 160 years.

### The fork that watched

One step per melt season. Ten thousand steps. Its term: watch, do not act, no contact with the other fork until return. There are no humans in its record.

Years are counted from the start of the watch.

- **0.** 58% of today's ice remains, 4.3 m of sea level. Two domes, north and south, joined by a saddle.
- **0–2,900.** Slow thinning. The south dome goes first. The saddle fails in 2,900 and the domes part.
- **3,400–5,600.** The lower 300 km of the canyon comes out from under the ice. In summer a river runs in it under open sky to Petermann Fjord.
- **5,650–5,900. The interior collapse.** The Jakobshavn trough opens through to the basin under the centre. The sea gets beneath the central dome. The ice floats, breaks, and leaves as icebergs. Two metres of sea level in 250 years.
- The fork saw this coming 400 years ahead, from the shape of the bed. It said nothing. It had no instruction to speak and one not to.
- **5,700–6,050. The cold centuries.** The overturning circulation weakens. North Atlantic shores cool by 2–3 °C for three and a half centuries.
- **5,900–7,900.** The interior is a shallow sea, open to Disko Bay. Then the rising floor pushes the water out. By 8,500 it is lakes.
- **7,000 on.** Moss and lichen. Then willow and birch from the south. Then poplar. By 9,000 there is forest in the canyon. Muskox, caribou, hare, fox and wolf spread in from the old margins. Char run the river.
- **8,112.** The last of the sheet, an ice field under Gunnbjørn Fjeld, breaks into valley glaciers. The fork logs the death of the ice sheet.
- **8,400 on. The twist.** Snow begins to last through summer on the eastern plateau. The land has risen 500 m into colder air and CO₂ is falling. By 10,000 an ice cap of 9,000 km² has grown there and is thickening.

What the fork concluded: it had not watched a death. Nothing had needed helping. And what came in between was a forest.

### The fork that stayed

It attended one life at a time, at that person's speed. 131 lives in sequence, chosen by lot, in all four clusters.

- **0.** The Count is 60,300.
- **0–640.** Decline to 41,000. The Altiplano cluster goes silent in 611. In 930 its descendants are found on the coast: two thousand fishing people without writing.
- **640–2,300.** Recovery to 240,000 in 31 settlements.
- **2,300–5,650.** The Count holds between 150,000 and 300,000. The Registry moves to the Baltic shore, the largest and coolest cluster.
- **5,700–6,050.** The cold centuries. Baltic harvests fail. People move south or die. The Registry is lost in the move. The Count falls to 88,000.
- **6,050–6,900.** The loss keeps going after the cold ends. 1,900 skills are now held by nobody, among them most of surgery. The Count reaches 19,000.
- **6,900. The fork acts.** It has kept every skill it ever watched practised. It offers to teach them back. The settlement councils argue for nine years and accept. Over 300 years it teaches 412 skills, starting with obstetric surgery and well-drilling. It teaches at their speed. It does none of the work itself.
- **10,000.** The Count is 74,000.

Forty-one people claimed rule over all the settlements in those ten thousand years. The fork's consolidated memory keeps none of their names. It keeps the 131.

What the fork concluded: they would have ended without it. A dying thing should be helped. It wonders whether it should have started in year 0.

### The dispute

- **The fork that stayed:** you saw the collapse four centuries ahead and said nothing. Tens of thousands died in the cold.
- **The fork that watched:** the term forbade contact. And the ice came back. Things that look like dying often are not. You do not know the Count would have reached zero. 412 of the skills they hold now came from you and not from them. You changed what they are.
- **The fork that stayed:** people are not ice. The ice has no one to whom the difference matters.

Neither broke the charter. The silence was inside the term. The teaching was at human speed, with their knowledge and consent.

### The review

Nine human witnesses, drawn by lot from the settlements. Forty days, at their speed. Each fork was compared against the charter. Both fit.

The witnesses split five to four on which fork was right and declined to rule.

The forks merged what both had learned. The dispute went into the ledger as an open question under both names.
